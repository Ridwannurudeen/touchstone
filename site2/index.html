How it works

What Touchstone does

Touchstone compiles a fund issuer's own disclosures into machine-checkable controls. Each control cites a byte-exact span of the issuer's document. A deterministic engine evaluates the controls against freshly retrieved evidence, signs the result with Ed25519, and publishes it to a registry on X Layer — testnet and mainnet both. A consumer contract, AssetGate, is written to gate on that state — on the report's status, freshness, publisher lineage and pinned control-set root. It is live on X Layer testnet at 0xAac48DC261B04737FDCB101D5049395121034a83 and is not deployed on mainnet, so no AssetGate reads the mainnet registry in production today; the signed report and bundle remain the offline-verifiable artifacts.

01

Compile

A model reads the issuer's published documents and proposes controls. Each candidate cites the exact bytes it was drawn from. Deterministic gates screen every candidate; a human approves the set. Declined candidates are recorded, with reasons.

02

Evaluate

An engine retrieves fresh evidence and evaluates each control. It never guesses. When the evidence cannot support an answer, the control is reported unevaluable — and that is a result, not an omission.

03

Publish

The report is signed with Ed25519 and published to a registry contract on X Layer testnet. Anyone can download the signed bundle and verify the signature, the roots, and every control hash offline.

Why it abstains

Abstention is a decision

On its first live run, Touchstone evaluated the eight approved controls for Superstate's USTB. Five were satisfied. For three, no qualifying earlier capture existed, so their values were not evaluated. The engine did not fill the gap with an assumption. It published the asset as UNVERIFIABLE — signed, sequenced, permanent.

Anyone can build a thing that says CONFIRMED. This one tells you when it doesn't know.

The consumer side

And the gate refuses it

A report is only worth something if a contract will act on it. AssetGate is live on X Layer testnet, pointed at the registry, and it admits CONFIRMED only. Asked about USTB, it reads the latest report, sees UNVERIFIABLE, and declines.

AssetGate 0xAac48DC261B04737FDCB101D5049395121034a83

check(0x82287f63…54cc8)

false  ·  “status not allowed”

That refusal is the deployment’s purpose, not its failure. The mask could have been widened until the call returned allowed — and it would then be a consumer contract that accepts an asset nobody has verified, which is the thing a gate exists to prevent.

So the whole chain is on public chains and every link of it says no: an engine that would not assert a NAV it could not confirm twice, and a contract that would not act on the report that resulted. Neither was staged.
